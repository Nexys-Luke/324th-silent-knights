Flight discipline first

We fly procedure-accurate intercepts, strike packages, and close air support, with an emphasis on comms discipline and formation flying over arcade dogfighting. The large majority of sorties are flown in daylight, since visual formation work and target acquisition are easier to teach and critique when everyone can actually see the flight.

Training

Structured upgrade path

New pilots start in the F-16C syllabus: basic fighter maneuvers, formation, then weapons employment before being cleared for ops.

Operations

Scripted campaigns

Multi-month campaign arcs with briefings, tasking orders, and debriefs — flown as a squadron, not a server full of strangers.

Community

Low-drama, high standard

Small roster by design. We'd rather have eighteen committed pilots than a hundred who show up once.

Scheduling

Daytime-first calendar

Core flight nights run in the early evening so daylight missions are the default; occasional dawn or dusk sorties are scheduled separately and flagged well in advance.

Standards

Checkride culture

Every upgrade — wingman, element lead, flight lead — is signed off by an instructor after a checkride, not just time served.

Terrain

Rotating theaters

We rotate between the Caucasus, Syria, and Persian Gulf maps across campaigns, so pilots get comfortable planning around different terrain and threat pictures.

Aircraft we fly

Two primary airframes, each with its own training track and its own pilots who specialize in it.

Primary1 — Air to Air / Strike

F-16C Viper

Our default airframe for intercepts, BFM training, and daytime strike packages. Most new pilots qualify on the Viper first since the bulk of our weekly sorties are flown in it.

Primary2 — Strike / Carrier Ops

F/A-18C Hornet

Flown by pilots who complete carrier qualifications. Used for strike missions launched from the boat and for cross-training in a different weapons and radar system.

Training pipeline

Every pilot moves through the same four stages before being cleared for squadron operations. Each stage is signed off individually — there's no fixed timeline, you move on when you're ready.

Stage 1Basic QualificationStartup, nav, landings, radio procedure

About the squadron

The 324th Silent Knights started as a handful of pilots flying together casually and grew into a structured virtual squadron over several years.

Origin

Founded 2019

Started with four pilots flying weekend intercept training. The name and patch came later, once the group decided to formalize into a squadron.

Growth

From four to eighteen

Grew slowly through word of mouth and a handful of open recruiting pushes rather than mass server invites, keeping the roster close-knit.

Today

Two weekly flight days

Now runs a regular Thursday and Sunday schedule with campaign arcs, dedicated training nights, and an active instructor cadre.

Frequently asked

Do you fly at night?

Occasionally. Nearly all scheduled sorties are daytime. We'll occasionally run an optional dusk, night or low-light training session, always flagged separately on the schedule.

Do I need DCS experience?

Basic flight-model familiarity helps, but it's not required. Trainees go through Stage 1 orientation regardless of prior hours.

What do I need to fly with you?

DCS World, the F-16C or F/A-18C module, a working headset, and a Discord account. HOTAS is strongly recommended but not mandatory to start.

Am I limited on what airframes I can fly?

No, You may fly whatever airframe you prefer but we have limited teachers that are able to teach other jets rather than the F-16C and the F/A-18C

How much time does this take?

Two flight days a week, roughly two to three hours each including briefing and debrief. Training stages can be done at your own pace outside that.

Can I fly both airframes?

Yes, once you're mission qualified on one. Most dual-qualified pilots pick up the second airframe after six months to a year.

Is there a minimum age?

We ask that pilots be 13 or older, mainly because of the comms discipline and campaign commitment involved.
